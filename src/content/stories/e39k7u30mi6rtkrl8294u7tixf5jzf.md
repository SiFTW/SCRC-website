<p>
<strong><em>I was diagnosed with Ovarian Cancer in February 2024 after several visits to my GP with vague symptoms that were easily confused with menopausal symptoms.</em></strong>&nbsp; <br><br>It was only after a urine sample came back as clear from the lab (I saw the results on the NHS app) that I queried why I was still symptomatic and I was sent for a scan.&nbsp; Once 2 x tumours were seen on the ultrasound scan things moved quickly and I was diagnosed 2 days later.&nbsp; This is a very common story in diagnosis of ovarian cancer – it hides in plain sight.&nbsp; If I hadn’t had the NHS app or had my background knowledge as a nurse I feel I would have been diagnosed even later.&nbsp; As it is, I am stage IIIc/IV.&nbsp; 
</p><p>
My care has been excellent since I was diagnosed and after CT and MRI scans and a biopsy I started 3 weekly chemo on 15th March.&nbsp; I had debulking surgery on 12th June which was very tough but successful and I’ve since had 3 x further rounds of chemo.&nbsp; I’m waiting for my end of treatment CT scan to see how things are now.
</p><p>
The worst part of the treatment for me has without a doubt been losing my hair/eyebrows/eyelashes.&nbsp; I can’t express just how awful this is – and I’m really not a vain person!&nbsp; I look in the mirror and don’t recognise myself.&nbsp; Now my tummy has a huge scar too it’s hard to remember who I was before all this.&nbsp;&nbsp; I also had to come off my HRT when I was diagnosed as there is a possibility it had been feeding my tumours.&nbsp; Dealing with menopausal symptoms on top of cancer treatment isn’t easy and I have found that the oncologist and surgeon aren’t really interested in helping me with this.
</p><p>
I’ve been very lucky in that my partner, family and friends have been an amazing support.&nbsp; I’ve also found online forums very helpful too.&nbsp; Unfortunately my work terms and conditions meant that I was only entitled to 6 weeks full pay and 6 weeks half pay and so I had to work through my first 4 chemo sessions to enable me to take 14 weeks off following the surgery and last 3 rounds of chemo.&nbsp; This was very tough and meant that I wasn’t paid for the last 6 weeks of my sick leave.
</p><p>
<em>Sometimes I feel positive about the future and sometimes I just want to cry at how unfair it all is. Now my treatment has come to an end I feel a bit lost – cut loose from all the appointments and treatment. All I can think about is when it will be back – even though at my last oncology appointment I was told I’m in complete remission.&nbsp; I’ve joined a Life after Cancer 6 week course and that has been a very positive, life affirming experience and has encouraged me to make plans for the future.&nbsp; To start with I’m planning a fancy holiday (once my hair has grown back a bit!).</em>
</p><p>
<em>Mary</em>
